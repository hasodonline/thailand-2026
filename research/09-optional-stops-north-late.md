# Optional Cool Stops — Chiang Mai Legs, 22–26 Sep 2026
Family: 2 adults + 5 kids (10–14), no temples, loves food / nature / animals / quirky. **V** = verified this session (source noted). **U** = unverified / verify locally.

## LEG 1 — Mae Wang / Mae Sapok, Wednesday 23.9 (free day at Chai Lai Orchid)

**1. Mae Wang bamboo rafting — RUNNING in late September, and at its best/wildest** (V)
- Where: the rafting strip along the Mae Wang river at Ban Sop Win / Mae Win, ~10–15 min drive downhill from Chai Lai. You just show up — every riverside restaurant on the strip sells rafting (verified: mychiangmaitour.com).
- Status in Sept: operates "daily, all year"; local blog (Ada House CNX) confirms the river "runs fullest in and just after the rainy season" — late Sept = fast, fun water. Caveats (V): operators cancel after heavy overnight rain, and they advise against babies/toddlers in rainy season — irrelevant for kids 10–14, who are the ideal age. Life jackets provided; expect to get soaked.
- Price (V): 600 THB per raft, max 2 passengers + pole-man (so ~3 rafts for the family ≈ 1,800–2,400 THB). Duration 1–2 h depending on water speed. Hours 06:00–18:00.
- Why cool: punting a bamboo raft through jungle with splashy small rapids — the #1 add-on for this family. Note they may already float this river with Chai Lai; ask Chai Lai first — they book it in-house.

**2. Pha Chor canyon ("Grand Canyon of Chiang Mai"), Mae Wang National Park** (V — thainationalparks.com, updated Jun 2026)
- 30 m eroded cliff/pillar corridor, genuinely otherworldly — kids walk *between* the pillars.
- Hours: 08:30–16:30 daily, year-round. Fees: 100 THB adult / 50 THB child (3–14) — so ~450–550 THB family.
- Effort: only ~400 m from car park to the viewpoint/corridor; stairs into the gorge; easy for these kids. September note (U): trail is clay — slippery/muddy right after rain, and the orange color actually pops most in wet season. Bring shoes with grip. Small café at the car park, no real restaurant (V).
- Time: 45–60 min on site. Detour: ~40–45 min drive from Chai Lai (south side of Mae Wang NP) — the one "big" outing of the free day, pairs well with lunch back on the rafting strip.

**3. Mae Sapok waterfall** (V — Ada House CNX + Chai Lai)
- 5–10 min from the lodge (Chai Lai is literally in Mae Sapok village). "Short walk through the trees to tumbling water and pools cool enough to swim." Free, no fixed hours, 30–60 min. In late Sept it's full and thundering — swim only in the lower pools, judge current on the day. Zero-effort filler between included activities.

**4. Lunch — riverside bamboo-hut restaurants on the rafting strip** (V that they exist and double as rafting operators; individual names U)
- Simple Thai food (grilled chicken, som tam, fried rice, whole fish) eaten in stilted bamboo salas over the river; picture menus, ~60–120 THB/dish. This *is* the local Mae Wang experience — pick whichever hut looks busiest. Chai Lai's own riverside restaurant is the fallback.

**5. Coffee farms in Mae Sapok** — mostly a miss (U)
- The valley's Karen villages do grow arabica, but I could not verify any named visitor-facing coffee farm/café in Mae Sapok this session. Don't build plans around it; ask Chai Lai staff — small farm visits are arranged informally.

**Stretch option (only if they want action, ~35–40 min toward the city): Grand Canyon Water Park, Hang Dong** — inflatable aqua-park on a quarry lake, kids this age love it. Status/hours/prices unverified this session (site returned 401) — check before driving.

## LEG 2 — Thursday 24.9, Chai Lai 09:30 → Bua Tong → Chiang Dao

**1. Huay Tueng Thao lake — the lunch stop. Verdict: yes, do it.** (V — two blogs + Wikivoyage, edits Jan 2026)
- Position: at the *north end* of the canal road (route 121) — essentially ON their route to the 1001; turn-off to entrance is only 1.8 km (≈ +5 min each way). They'd pass it ~10:45–11:00; lakeside kitchens open from ~10:00 (V) — early-ish lunch works, or brunch-style.
- Entry (V): 50 THB/person foreigners (20 THB Thai); keep tickets for the checkpoint 200 m in. Gate hours ~07:00–18:00.
- The straw giants (V): "Huai Tueng Thao Sculpture Park" — a King Kong family in rice-straw: dad "Choke Larp" 8 m, mom "Kwan" 6 m, son 4 m, plus elephants, peacock, boar, buffalo and a giant gorilla *hand* viewpoint — kids can climb ladders inside the figures for photos. No extra fee. Exactly this family's brand of quirky.
- Food (V): ~a dozen Thai restaurants in thatched huts ON the water around the 4 km lake; picture menus, cheap and good ("rice + pork + fried egg" praised; frog for the brave). Walk the shore, choose a hut.
- Time: 60–90 min covers lunch + sculptures. This beats any Mae Jo café stop (nothing verifiable there worth a detour).
- Timing math: HTT 10:45–12:15 → Bua Tong ~13:10 → climb till ~14:45 → Chiang Dao ~16:00 (30 min later than their 15:30 target — acceptable, or keep lunch to 60 min).

**2. At Bua Tong itself** (V core facts — Wikivoyage Dec 2025)
- Entry FREE; reached 3 km off road 1001 after km-stone 48 (signed). Climbing the mineral-coated "sticky" limestone barefoot is the whole event — grippy even in wet season, though the top step after rain can run harder; hold the ropes on the steepest bits.
- On-site facilities (U): commonly reported food/drink stalls, toilets and changing rooms at the top picnic area, plus the turquoise Chet Si spring pool a short walk away — I could not re-verify the stalls this session; don't rely on it for lunch (another reason HTT lunch first is right). No notable café worth naming at the falls.

**3. Alternative/backup lunch — Chom Cafe & Restaurant, Mae Hia** (V — official site)
- Famous misty "moss garden" jungle-fantasy restaurant (waterfalls, fog machines, koi) — massive kid wow-factor. Open daily 11:00–22:00, tel 053-271-749. BUT: it's on the Canal Rd (700 Years Rd) at Mae Hia, which they pass ~10:15 — before opening. Only works if they leave Chai Lai ~10:15 and make Chom the 11:00 early lunch (adds ~45 min to the day). Otherwise save this name for any other Chiang Mai city day. Prices mid-range (~150–300 THB/dish, U).

## LEG 3 — Chiang Dao / Mae Mae, Thursday evening + early Friday

**1. Chiang Dao Nest 1 restaurant — YES, open to non-guests, and worth it** (V — chiangdaonest.com)
- The famous kitchen of chef-owner Wicha (trained Norwich City College UK, 2000–02); gourmet European 3-course menus that change periodically, some Thai dishes, vegetarian options. **Dinner 18:00–21:30 daily; children's menu (12 & under) served till 20:30; advance reservation recommended** — book: 053-456612 / nest@chiangdao.com. Prices not published (U; expect ~300–600 THB/main). Location: ~1 km before Chiang Dao Cave on the same dead-end road — i.e. ~10 min drive from Mae Mae village / Tree House Hideaway (U on exact minutes). This is the obvious Thursday dinner: arrive THH 15:30, settle in, 18:00 dinner at Nest 1.
- Note: kids 10–14 mostly count as adults for the menu — fine, the food is the point.

**2. Chiang Dao Cave — the lantern tour** (V — Wikivoyage)
- 40 THB/person for the 350 m lit section (self-guided); the magic is the local guides with gas lanterns waiting along the lit path: **200 THB per group of up to 5** for the unlit section — crawling through narrows, bats, an "elephant" and "upside-down lotus" stalagmite. Their 7 people = 2 groups/2 lanterns ≈ 400 THB + entries ≈ 680 THB total. Kids 10–14: perfect (some agility needed).
- Hours: closing time NOT verified this session — typically ~16:30–17:00 last entry (U). Arriving THH 15:30 makes a same-afternoon cave dash *possible but tight*; safer to confirm closing time via Nest (053-456612) when booking dinner. It cannot move to Friday (07:45 departure precedes opening).
- 5 km from Chiang Dao town, ~10 min from Mae Mae; it's at a temple entrance (fish pond with giant carp at the stairs — feed them, 10–20 THB fish food) but the visit is a cave, not a temple — fits the no-temples rule.

**3. Chiangdao Hot Spring (Ban Tham)** (V — Wikivoyage)
- Japanese-garden bathing pools: basic concrete basins free; landscaped Japanese Garden area 100 THB (changing rooms, showers, gazebos, small restaurant). Close to the cave road (~7 km from the Nests per their own site). Nice 45–60 min soak Thursday ~16:30–17:30 before dinner if the cave doesn't fit. Hours not posted (U — daylight hours). The bigger Pong Arng Hot Spring (100 THB foreigners, 08:00–18:00) is 35 km NORTH — wrong direction, skip.
- Viewpoints: the classic Doi Luang Chiang Dao wall looms over Mae Mae itself — sunset from the Tree House Hideaway deck likely beats any drive; San Phakia viewpoint is 24 km of dirt road — skip.
- Realistic plan: Thu 15:30 arrive → either cave-with-lanterns (if hours confirmed) or hot-spring soak → 18:00 Nest 1 dinner. Fri: just leave 07:45; morning mist on the massif from the car is the show.

## LEG 4 — Friday 25.9, rafting → Raya Heritage + final evening

**1. Wat Ban Den (Wat Den Salee Sri Muang Gan), Mae Taeng — honest verdict: mention, don't push.** (V existence/features — Wikipedia; hours U)
- It IS a jaw-dropper — a hillside city of turquoise-roofed Lanna buildings, 12 zodiac stupas, giant naga staircases — and it's only ~10 min off route 107 near Inthakhin, ~15–20 min from the 8Adventures base, directly between rafting and Raya. Free (donation); usually open ~06:00–17:00/18:00 (U); 15–30 min is enough for the photo-wow.
- But: it is unambiguously a temple. For a family that dislikes temples, on a day when they'll be wet and tired at 13:30, I'd flag it as "the one temple that looks like a fantasy film set — 15-minute leg-stretch if anyone's curious," and expect them to skip. Nothing else along Mae Taeng's 107 (viewpoints are up the 1095 toward Pai — wrong direction) earns a stop; no verifiable stand-out café on 107 either (U). Better plan: drive straight, arrive Raya 15:00, and use **Raya Heritage's own gorgeous riverside café/lounge** for the post-rafting coffee (on-site, zero minutes).

**2. Final evening from Raya Heritage (Don Kaeo, Mae Rim — Ping riverside, ~12–15 min north of Charoenrat Rd)**
- **The Riverside Bar & Restaurant** (V — official site + Wikivoyage): 9–11 Charoenrat Rd, "the legend of the Ping" since 1984; Thai + Western; live music from ~19:00 (Eagles/Beatles-ish early set — family-safe timing; rockier after 21:00); gets crowded — come ~18:30 or book +66 5324 3239. Quirky bonus the kids will vote for: **nightly dinner-cruise boat at 20:00, +110 THB/person** — dinner on a boat on the Ping. Mains ~150–350 THB (U).
- **The Good View** (V — Wikivoyage): 13 Charoenrat Rd, next door. Daily 10:00–01:00; 150+ Thai/Chinese/Western dishes incl. pizza (safe for mixed kid tastes); live music evenings; queues nightly — arrive early. Tel +66 53-241886.
- **Woo Cafe** — could NOT verify; multiple signals it may have closed permanently (site 403). Do not plan around it.
- **Night Bazaar** (V hours — Wikivoyage): daily 18:00–24:00, Changklan Rd. From the riverside restaurants it's a 10–12 min walk across Nawarat Bridge (or 5 min drive; 15 min direct from Raya). Verdict: yes — it's the sensible last-evening souvenir run: T-shirts, textiles, caps, gadgets for five kids in one dense sweep, plus Anusarn food market. Touristy and repetitive (manage expectations), but exactly right for a final-night souvenir mission before the 08:00 airport run. Combined plan: 18:00 dinner Riverside/Good View → 20:00–21:30 bazaar → back to Raya by 22:00.
